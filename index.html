About

I’m a data scientist at UCSF Health, where I build, evaluate, and monitor the AI and machine-learning tools that support patient care and hospital operations.

My work sits where machine learning, simulation, and operations research meet: turning complex clinical and operational data into models that clinicians and leaders can act on. At UCSF, that includes large language models for clinical summarization, real-time bed-availability prediction, and the monitoring and evaluation of AI models in production.

Before UCSF, I was a healthcare data scientist at the University of Maryland Medical System, where my discrete-event simulations cut emergency department wait times by 2–4 hours and my COVID-19 forecasting models outperformed external benchmarks. My doctoral research at Virginia Tech, funded by the Ohio Department of Medicaid, used predictive and system-dynamics models to understand infant mortality and preterm birth and to inform policy for high-risk populations.

I’m driven by the potential of data to make healthcare more efficient, equitable, and patient-centered — and I’m always glad to connect with people working toward the same goal.
